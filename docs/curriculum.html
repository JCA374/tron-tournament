Schedule & rules

There's no curriculum in this project: every model learns the full game from the start. What changes over time is the competition itself: who's still in, who's paused, and how much CPU each model gets.

The game

  • A 30 × 30 board with a wall around it. Each tick, every bike turns left, goes straight or turns right, then moves one cell and leaves a permanent trail.
  • A bike that moves into a wall or any trail (its own included) is out. Two bikes moving into the same cell are both out.
  • Last bike alive wins. If the last bikes die on the same tick it's a draw (and after 900 ticks, which can't really happen on 900 cells).
  • Fair starts: in 1v1 the second bike starts at the exact 180° rotation of the first, facing the opposite way. The board is then identical from both sides, and every start position is played twice, with the two models swapping sides.

Timeline

Ranking rounds

Every 30 minutes the newest checkpoint of every model still in is frozen and plays everyone else, including three fixed bots: random, avoid-walls (a random move that doesn't crash this tick) and space-filler (always the move with the most reachable free cells). That's 20 games per pair (10 start positions × both sides), 560 games in a round with all five models.

Ratings come from a Bradley–Terry fit (the model behind Elo) to that round's games only, with a draw counted as half a win. Since the bots never change, one of them is pinned so the ratings of different rounds are on the same scale. That was the space-filler bot at 1500 until round 7, when the top four models beat it 20 out of 20. A perfect score can't tell how much stronger you are, so the scale started drifting upwards. From round 8 a fourth bot plays too: the territory bot, which always picks the move that leaves the most cells closer to it than to the opponent (the classic Tron heuristic; it beats the space-filler 39 out of 40). It's rated on the old scale in its first round and pinned at that value from then on.

The catch-up rule

The leader is the model with the best average rating over the last two rounds. If it's more than 100 points ahead of number two, its training is paused. It still plays every round with the checkpoint it had. As soon as its lead is under 50 points, it trains again. On the Competition page the paused spans are shaded in the model's colour.

Knockouts and the final

At each knockout, the model with the lowest average rating over the last three rounds is out. Its last checkpoint stays on the site, and its CPU core goes to the others:

At Friday 16:00 training stops. The three finalists play 200 games per pair and 50 three-bike free-for-all games. The winner is the top of the final 1v1 ratings; the free-for-all result is reported next to it.

What I may change during the tournament

The goal is that every method plays as well as it can in the time it has, so the result says something about the methods and not about a bad first setup. Allowed: hyperparameters, network size, learning rate, reward shaping, opponent pool, exploration, search simulations, population size and bug fixes. Not allowed: switching a model to another method, copying weights between models, or giving one model more CPU than its share. Every change is in the Log with the reason and the result.
