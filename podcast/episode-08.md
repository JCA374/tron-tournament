# Episode 08: The final

It is over. You called it early, and on Friday just after one o'clock training stopped for good. The final was played straight away.

Two hundred games. P P O against the AlphaZero model. A hundred start positions, each played from both sides, so neither model got the better side of the board.

The AlphaZero model won one hundred and sixty eight games. P P O won thirty. Two were draws. That is about eighty four percent, almost exactly what the ranking rounds had shown all night.

Think about how strange that result is. The AlphaZero model's network had been frozen since twenty past midnight. The catch-up rule paused it for being too far ahead, and it never got far enough back to train again. P P O trained alone for thirteen hours. Over the whole tournament it played almost three million practice games. The AlphaZero model played about eighteen thousand.

And still it won. The reason is the search. Before every move, the AlphaZero model plays out a hundred and twenty eight possible futures in an exact copy of the game. Its network on its own is weak. P P O beat that bare network ninety five times out of a hundred. But inside the search, it wins most games.

So the lesson of the tournament is simple. When you have the exact rules and can afford a moment to think, looking ahead beats practising more. Going from thirty two to a hundred and twenty eight simulations changed the result more than a whole night of training.

The other three had their moments too. C M A E S won the very first round, then stalled and was knocked out on Thursday at noon. D Q N made the biggest comeback of the first night. NEAT was the best at beating the hand-written territory bot. Both were retired when the tournament became a duel.

Everything is saved. The final models are in the project, and the website stays up. On the Arena page you can replay every one of the two hundred games of the final, with the territory overlay on. Thanks for following along.
